Relo: Corporate Relocation Onboarding Platform
1. Problem Statement
Relocating employees need a way to settle into a new city without stitching together housing,
local services, and orientation from scattered broker apps and word of mouth, because what
actually holds them back isn't a shortage of options, it's the absence of a source they can trust
from day one.
2. User Persona
Persona A, End User: Rohan Verma, 28, Senior Analyst, just transferred from Pune to
Bangalore and joins in three weeks. He doesn't know anyone in the city yet. His biggest worry is
finding a safe place near oƯice and figuring out how long it'll take to feel settled. His current plan
is "figure it out once I land there," which isn't really a plan. He won't go looking for five diƯerent
apps to solve five diƯerent problems, but if his company gave him one app on day one, he would
use it without a second thought. It's not that the information doesn't exist, it's that none of it
comes with anyone vouching for it, and that's the part he's actually missing.
Persona B, Buyer: Ananya Krishnan, 34, HR Manager for Talent Mobility, handles 40 to 50
transfers a year with a fixed allowance, an emailed checklist, and some informal help from
employees already living in that city. She knows this isn't enough since relocation issues have
come up in exit interviews before, but she doesn't have the budget for a full relocation desk
outside leadership moves. She wants something she can plug straight into onboarding,
something that reduces the constant back and forth queries to her team, and something she
can point to as an actual benefit when convincing someone to accept a transfer.
3. Rationale
Relo is built B2B because the incentives simply work better on the employer side. An employee
who relocates once every few years has little reason to pay for or go find a dedicated app on his
own, but he'll use one in a heartbeat if it shows up already endorsed by his company. That's
really the whole insight behind this: the missing piece for Rohan was never information, it was
trust, and trust is something an employer can hand over instantly in a way a random app never
can. HR, on the other hand, treats relocation as a recurring, budgeted problem, so Ananya is
essentially paying to cut attrition risk, reduce manual queries, and make the transfer oƯer more
attractive. This also gives us a cleaner path to market. Instead of acquiring relocating employees
one at a time, which is slow and low trust, we sell to HR teams as a single onboarding decision,
and employees end up using it automatically every time a relocation happens.
